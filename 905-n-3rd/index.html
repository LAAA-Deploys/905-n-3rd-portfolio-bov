Buyer Profile & Transaction Strategy
Who Is Most Likely to Buy, and How We Create Competition

Market Context

Six Burbank apartment sales of 11 to 24 units closed between December 2025 and September 2026 at $264,643 to $380,769 per unit and 10.56 to 13.44 times gross rent. The closest in age and size, 1799 N Hollywood Way, 13 units built in 1963, closed at $3.45M in December 2025, $265,385 per unit, a 6.26% cap on its own income. 233-237 N Hollywood Way, 14 units, closed at $3.705M, a 5.95% cap, and 615 E Magnolia Boulevard, 11 units, at $3.0M, a 6.66% cap.

Thirty days before each of those sales closed, the 5-year Treasury ran 3.6% to 4.4%. On 09/18/2026 it stood at 4.86% (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis, FRED series DGS5).

A buyer today pays more for money than the buyers who set those comps. That is why the recommended price sits at a 6.0% cap, at the middle of the comparable yields rather than the low end of them.

Positioning Thesis

The right buyer wants a fully occupied 31-unit building with a new roof, rents 8.6% under market and a Downtown Burbank corner that the city is planning for more density. That buyer should be qualified early on how they will handle the soft-story question and the building's financing, because those decide whether an offer holds through escrow.

Likely Buyer Profiles

Likely Buyer 01

1031 Exchange Buyer

Exchange buyers who need a replacement property of this size on a 45-day identification clock. A fully occupied building with a new roof is simple to underwrite on a deadline.

Likely Buyer 02

Burbank and Valley Apartment Owner

Owners who already hold apartments in Burbank and the east Valley, including the buyers of the sales in this analysis. They know the tenant base and buy on yield and price per unit.

Likely Buyer 03

Long-Horizon Corner Buyer

Buyers who hold for the long term and want the whole corner, pricing today's income while the city finishes its Downtown TOD plan.

Transaction Strategy

01

Price Each Building on Today's Rates

The Burbank sales in this analysis priced when the 5-year Treasury ran 3.6% to 4.4%; it stood at 4.86% on 09/18/2026. Each building is priced on its own income at a yield today's buyer can finance, so the offering draws offers in the first weeks rather than sitting while the market catches up to the ask.

02

Market the Corner as One Offering

The two parcels are one 0.83 acre corner at N 3rd Street and E Burbank Boulevard. We recommend offering them together with a separate price for each building, and taking offers on either or both, so each entity closes its own sale and the buyer who wants the whole corner can buy it.

The LAAA Team of Marcus & Millichap is pleased to present 905 N 3rd Street, a 31-unit apartment building in Downtown Burbank. The two-story wood frame building was built in 1972 and holds 27 one bedroom and 4 two bedroom units, 20,281 square feet on a 26,397 square foot lot. Tenants share a pool and a fitness room. The building is covered by AB 1482 and Burbank's Tenant Protection Ordinance.

The investment case is a fully occupied building with its roof work done. Scheduled rent sits about 8.6% under market, and a new TPO roof was permitted on the main building in November 2024.

The building shares its corner with 237-239 E Burbank Boulevard, which is offered alongside it. The offering fits 1031 buyers and long-term Burbank owners.

Location Overview
Downtown Burbank, at N 3rd Street and E Burbank Boulevard

The property sits at N 3rd Street and E Burbank Boulevard on the northwest edge of Downtown Burbank. Burbank Town Center is 0.35 miles southeast and McCambridge Park 0.5 miles northwest, and Walk Score rates the block 91 for its access to daily errands.

The Downtown Burbank Metrolink station is 0.8 miles southeast, and the NoHo to Pasadena bus rapid transit line stops on Olive Avenue about 0.6 miles away. Hollywood Burbank Airport is 2.0 miles west. Walt Disney Studios is 2.2 miles south, Providence Saint Joseph Medical Center 2.3 miles south and Warner Bros. Studios 2.9 miles southwest.

ZIP 91502 is 90% renter-occupied, with a median household income of $52,473 and a median gross rent of $1,916. The city's draft Downtown TOD Specific Plan, dated 06/23/2026, would raise this block to 87 units per acre, with hearings targeted for late fall 2026.
